Part 01

The Problem — Why Mining Safety Needs AI

Mines are among the most dangerous workplaces on Earth. Every year, collisions between workers on foot and massive machines cause fatal accidents that are entirely preventable.

Picture a CAT 793F haul truck — it weighs over 200 tonnes and stands as tall as a two-storey building. The driver sitting in the cabin has blind spots stretching up to 50 metres in every direction. A person standing just 15 metres behind the truck is completely invisible to the driver. Now imagine this scenario in thick dust clouds, dim underground tunnels, or during a 12-hour night shift when fatigue sets in.

The Four Core Challenges

🔲 Blind Spots

Heavy mining vehicles have enormous blind zones. Drivers simply cannot see workers nearby from their elevated cabins, especially behind and below the vehicle.

🌫️ Poor Visibility

Open-pit mines produce thick dust clouds. Underground mines have near-zero natural light. Rain, fog, and glare make visual awareness unreliable.

😴 Operator Fatigue

Workers and drivers operate 12-hour shifts in extreme heat, cold, or noise. Fatigue impairs reaction time, decision-making, and situational awareness.

📡 Communication Gaps

A safety supervisor stationed 2 km away in the control room has zero real-time awareness of what's happening on the pit floor. By the time a radio call reaches them, it's often too late.

The core question: How do you keep workers safe when the driver can't see them, dust blocks all visibility, everyone is exhausted, and the supervisor is kilometres away?

Part 02

Our Solution — What HALOCAS Does

HALOCAS is a smart camera-based safety system that watches for danger, identifies who is at risk, and instantly alerts the right supervisor — all automatically, all in real time.

Think of HALOCAS as an always-awake safety guard mounted on every piece of heavy machinery. It uses cameras (instead of expensive radar or RFID tags) to continuously monitor the area around the machine. When a worker walks too close, the system:

Detects the Worker

AI-powered computer vision spots every person and every machine in the camera feed — even in poor lighting or dusty conditions.

Calculates the Distance

The system continuously measures how far each worker is from the machine and whether they are moving closer (a "closing trajectory").

Recognises the Person

Using facial recognition, HALOCAS identifies who the worker is — their name, role, department, and who their supervisor is.

Captures Video Evidence

A 5-second video clip of the incident is automatically saved — showing exactly what happened, with bounding boxes and distance labels overlaid.

Alerts the Supervisor

An email is sent immediately to the worker's direct supervisor with the video clip, the worker's name, the distance measured, and the exact timestamp.

In simple terms: HALOCAS is like giving every piece of heavy machinery its own pair of intelligent eyes and a direct phone line to the safety supervisor.

Part 03

How It Works — Step by Step

The system processes every single camera frame — 30 times per second — through a multi-stage pipeline. Here's what happens inside.

Stage 1 — Sensing

Cameras Capture the Scene

Front and rear cameras mounted on the machine stream live video at 1080p resolution, 30 frames per second. This raw video is the starting point for everything.

Stage 2 — Detection

AI Spots People and Machines

Each frame is processed by YOLOv8 (You Only Look Once, version 8) — one of the fastest and most accurate object detection models in the world. It draws a box around every person and every vehicle it sees, and assigns each one a unique tracking ID using ByteTrack, so it can follow them across frames.

Stage 3 — Safety Evaluation

The Distance Calculator

The Safety State Machine takes the detected positions and calculates the distance between each worker and each machine. It also computes the closing velocity — whether the gap is shrinking. Based on this, every worker-machine pair is classified into one of three safety states:

  • ✓ SAFE (Green): Worker is more than 10 metres away — no action needed.
  • ✓ WARNING (Amber): Worker is between 3 and 10 metres away — the system watches closely.
  • ✓ CRITICAL (Red): Worker is under 3 metres OR is moving towards the machine — immediate alert triggered.

Stage 4 — Identification

Who Is at Risk?

When a CRITICAL alert fires, DeepFace (with the Facenet512 model) extracts the worker's face from the video frame and compares it against all enrolled workers in the database. It generates a 512-dimensional mathematical fingerprint of the face — unique to each person — and matches it in milliseconds. This tells the system exactly who is in danger and which supervisor to notify.

Stage 5 — Response

Automatic Alert with Evidence

Three things happen simultaneously: a 5-second video clip is trimmed from the rolling buffer and uploaded to cloud storage; an email with the clip attached is dispatched to the worker's supervisor; and the incident is permanently logged in the database with full details — distance, velocity, severity, timestamp, and worker identity.

Part 04

System Architecture — The Four Layers

HALOCAS is organised into four clean layers, each with a specific job. Think of it like a building — each floor handles one part of the work.

📷

Layer 1 — Edge / On-Machine

Front camera, rear camera, and optional radar sensors physically mounted on each piece of heavy machinery. These capture raw video and send it to the processing server.

⚙️

Layer 2 — Processing Server (Backend on Render)

The brain of the system. Runs YOLOv8 detection, ByteTrack tracking, the safety state machine, DeepFace recognition, video clip trimming, and notification dispatch — all inside a FastAPI application.

☁️

Layer 3 — Storage (Cloud Free Tiers)

Neon PostgreSQL stores all worker profiles, machine records, and incident logs. Cloudflare R2 stores video clips. Resend handles email delivery. All at zero cost on their generous free tiers.

🖥️

Layer 4 — Dashboard (Frontend on Vercel)

A beautiful Next.js web application where supervisors can see live camera feeds, monitor active alerts, review incident history, manage worker profiles, and configure safety thresholds.

Why this design? Each layer can be updated, scaled, or replaced independently. The camera doesn't care about the database. The dashboard doesn't care about how detection works. This is called separation of concerns and it makes the system robust and maintainable.

Key Features of the System

Every feature was designed to solve a specific real-world problem in mine safety operations.

Spatial Proximity Detection

YOLOv8 neural tracking of workers and vehicles in real time, with continuous distance calculation between every worker-machine pair.

  • ✓ Detection: Identifies people, trucks, excavators, and loaders
  • ✓ Tracking: ByteTrack assigns persistent IDs across frames
  • ✓ Distance: Real-time monocular distance estimation
YOLOv8 ByteTrack 30 FPS

Biometric Verification

DeepFace (Facenet512) authorised operator recognition — the system knows who is near the machine, not just that someone is near it.

  • ✓ 512-D Embeddings: Facial geometry fingerprint, not colour matching
  • ✓ Authorised Override: Known mechanics can suppress alerts
  • ✓ Accuracy: 99.65% on LFW benchmark
DeepFace Facenet512 RetinaFace

Lazy-Proxy Memory Safety

DeepFace + TensorFlow can consume 350 MB on import alone. HALOCAS uses lazy proxy loading — the heavy models are loaded only when the first face needs to be verified, not at startup.

  • ✓ Startup: Near-instant boot, no 350 MB TensorFlow allocation
  • ✓ Memory: Keeps idle RAM usage minimal
  • ✓ Free Tier Safe: Fits within Render's 512 MB RAM limit
Lazy Loading Proxy Pattern

Telemetry Streaming

Real-time async WebSocket channels broadcast safety events, distances, and velocities to the dashboard — supervisors see alerts the instant they happen.

  • ✓ WebSocket: Live push to all connected dashboard clients
  • ✓ Email: Supervisor alerts via Resend API with cooldown
  • ✓ Cooldown: 60-second suppression per worker-machine pair

The worker directory displays all enrolled mine personnel with their role, department, biometric enrollment status (512-D Enrolled), hazard zone authorisation level, and breach history. Each worker has a profile "dossier" with their full incident history. Supports grid and table views.

Fleet Management

Tracks all heavy equipment in the fleet — haul trucks, excavators, loaders — with their current operational zone, telemetry speed, number of mounted sensors, and online status (Active, Standby, or Maintenance). Shows 10 total fleet assets across multiple pit sectors.

Settings & Calibration

Safety thresholds are fully configurable: Critical Safety Halo Distance (default: 3 metres), Warning Safety Halo Distance (default: 10 metres), Camera Pixel-To-Metre Calibration Constant, Debounce Confirmation Window (how many consecutive frames must confirm danger before triggering), Alert Cooldown Window (60 seconds), and the Supervisor Email Gateway address for automated notifications.

Part 08

The Journey — From Prototype to Production

HALOCAS didn't start as a production system. It began as a rough prototype with significant gaps, and was methodically transformed into a fully deployable, production-grade platform.

What the Prototype Already Had

The original codebase was a solid proof of concept — it demonstrated that the core idea worked. It included:

  • ✓ YOLOv8 object detection with ByteTrack multi-object tracking
  • ✓ A three-state safety machine (Safe → Warning → Critical)
  • ✓ A rolling video buffer that could export incident clips
  • ✓ A basic SQLite database for workers and incidents
  • ✓ Async email sending via SMTP
  • ✓ 24 unit tests covering all modules
  • ✓ A single-page HTML dashboard with MJPEG streaming

What Was Missing (and What We Built)

The prototype had a critical weakness and several production gaps. Here's what changed:

Critical Fix
Real Face Recognition

The prototype used HSV colour histograms — essentially matching people by the colour of their hard hat. Two workers wearing the same coloured helmet would be confused. We replaced this with DeepFace (Facenet512), which creates a 512-dimensional mathematical map of actual facial geometry. Accuracy jumped from unreliable to 99.65%.

Infrastructure
Production Database & Cloud

SQLite was replaced with Neon PostgreSQL for concurrent access and proper migrations. Video clips now upload to Cloudflare R2 for permanent, zero-egress storage. Email was switched from localhost SMTP to the Resend API for reliable delivery.

Interface
Professional Dashboard

The basic HTML page was replaced with a full Next.js 14 application featuring six pages: Dashboard, Live Monitoring, Incidents, Workers, Machines, and Settings — all with responsive design, real-time data, and professional dark-themed UI.

How was the code written? The entire production codebase was engineered using Antigravity IDE powered by Gemini 3.7 Flash — an AI coding agent. The production blueprint (a 45-page document) contained sequential prompts, and the AI agent executed each one phase by phase, with automated testing and validation at every step.

EMESRT Safety Classification

The mining industry uses the EMESRT (Earth Moving Equipment Safety Round Table) framework to classify CAS maturity from Level 1 (basic awareness) to Level 9 (fully autonomous). HALOCAS targets Level 6–7 — active advisory with AI-powered identification and automatic supervisor notification. The face recognition component adds an intelligence layer that no commercial CAS currently offers at this price point.

Appendix

Glossary — Simple Definitions

Technical terms used in this document, explained in plain language.

CAS (Collision Avoidance System)
A safety technology that prevents crashes between workers and heavy machines.
PDS (Proximity Detection System)
A system that measures and alerts when objects get too close to each other.
YOLOv8
"You Only Look Once" — an AI model that can detect objects (people, vehicles) in images extremely fast.
ByteTrack
A method that assigns each detected object a unique ID so it can be followed across multiple video frames.
DeepFace
An open-source face recognition library that can identify individuals from their facial features.
Facenet512
A specific face recognition model that creates a 512-number mathematical "fingerprint" of each face.
FastAPI
A modern Python framework for building high-performance web servers and APIs.
Next.js
A popular framework for building fast, interactive websites using React.
PostgreSQL / Neon
A powerful database system for storing structured data. Neon is a free cloud-hosted version.
WebSocket
A technology that allows the server to push live updates to the dashboard instantly, without refreshing the page.
Cloudflare R2
A cloud storage service for files (like video clips) that's free for up to 10 GB.
CI/CD
Continuous Integration / Continuous Deployment — an automated system that tests code and publishes updates whenever changes are made.
EMESRT
Earth Moving Equipment Safety Round Table — an industry body that defines safety maturity levels for mining equipment.
Safety State Machine
A logical system with defined states (Safe, Warning, Critical) that transitions between them based on distance and movement rules.
Closing Velocity
The speed at which two objects are moving towards each other — i.e., how fast the gap is shrinking.
Embedding
A list of numbers (like a mathematical fingerprint) that represents something — here, it represents a person's face in a way that computers can compare.
